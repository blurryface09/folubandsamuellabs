## 3. The three modes — read this before §4

The mistake to avoid is walking into every meeting as the small company asking for a favour. As a
services company with our own products and a training academy, we sit on **three** sides of each of
these relationships:

| Mode | Direction | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — We sell to them** | They are our **client**. Revenue in. | CoralPay is a licensed payment switch: pen testing, PCI-adjacent security work and outsourced engineers are recurring, mandatory spend for them |
| **B — We buy or integrate from them** | They are our **vendor/enabler**. Cost down, capability up. | CoralPay's USSD and bulk-payment rails inside TailorNow and our payroll product |
| **C — We go to market together** | They are our **channel**, we are theirs. Revenue shared. | Snapnet introduces us to their enterprise clients for work they don't do; we refer cloud deals to their CSP |

**Lead with Mode A or C. Ask for Mode B second.** An offer establishes us as a peer; an ask alone
establishes us as a supplicant. Most of these companies are worth more to us as clients or channels
than as sponsors.

### 5.1 CoralPay — payment rails, and a security client

- **Website:** http://www.coralpay.com · **LinkedIn:** https://ng.linkedin.com/company/coralpay-technology-nig-limited
- **HQ:** Lagos · **Confidence:** High

**What they do.** CoralPay Technology (Nig) Ltd is a payment technology company operating in Nigeria
since the mid-2000s, licensed by the CBN as a Payment Solutions Service Provider (PSSP), a non-bank
acquirer, and — since 2018 — a **payment switching company**. Their stated vision is to be "the gold
standard for payment and processing in Africa".

**Their products, mapped to our portfolio:**

| CoralPay product | Where it lands in FSLabs |
| --- | --- |
| **C'Gate USSD** | TailorNow checkout with no data/no card — recovers customers who abandon card payment |
| **NQR** (QR payments) | A tailor takes an in-person deposit by QR that lands inside our order record — bridges offline walk-ins into the platform |
| **Pay-With-Bank-Transfer** | Nigeria's highest-trust consumer method — TailorNow and Exchange |
| **Fast Channel** (bulk payments) | **Payroll disbursement for our HR platform**, marketplace payouts, group/uniform orders. This is the biggest single fit. |
| **Instant Pay** (interbank transfers) | Exchange settlement and creative payouts |
| **Gateway, card issuing & acquiring** | Second rail; non-bank acquiring may price better |
| **VAS API** (bill payments) | Optional revenue line inside our consumer products |

**Why this is our strongest lead.** Payments touch three of our four products. And a licensed switch
has **mandatory, recurring security spend** — penetration testing, security assessments, secure
development review — which is precisely Samuel's discipline. So CoralPay is plausibly both our
vendor and our client.

**What we offer (lead with this).**

1. **Cybersecurity services** — penetration testing and security assessment for a licensed switch,
   where testing isn't optional and re-testing is periodic.
2. **Outsourced engineering capacity** — integration engineers, QA, DevOps without headcount.
3. **Academy as their talent pipeline** — a co-branded or sponsored cohort producing engineers
   already trained on their APIs. Developers who know your rails ship on your rails.
4. **A merchant category they cannot reach one-by-one** — thousands of cash-based tailoring
   businesses, pre-verified and KYC'd through TailorNow, via a single integration.
5. **A flagship USSD/NQR financial-inclusion case study** — the story a switch wants in regulator and
   press conversations.

**What we ask.**

1. **Merchant/PSSP terms priced against our aggregated volume** across TailorNow's creatives, rather
   than each tailor priced as a standalone micro-merchant.
2. **C'Gate USSD and NQR live in TailorNow checkout**, alongside card and transfer.
3. **Fast Channel bulk-payout access** for payroll disbursement and marketplace settlement.
4. Sandbox access, a named technical contact, and integration support.
5. Their compliance view on our escrow-style marketplace flow — free expertise, and it flatters them.

**Watch-outs.** TailorNow already runs on Paystack with automatic split settlement, so frame the ask
as *adding reach* (USSD/QR/bulk payout) first and re-pricing second — a migration has real cost.
Critically: confirm whether their model settles **automatically to the tailor's own account** or
whether we take custody and disburse. A custody model changes our regulatory position and
reconciliation burden and is worth avoiding. Separately, our Exchange product touches crypto — know
how they feel about that **before** raising it, so it doesn't contaminate the core conversation.

**Team brainstorm prompts.**
- What share of TailorNow checkout drop-off would USSD plausibly recover? Do we have funnel numbers
  to quote?
- Is dual-rail (Paystack + CoralPay) worth the reconciliation complexity, or do we pick one per
  product — Paystack for marketplace, CoralPay for payroll?
- Do we lead with security services or with the merchant story? (One makes us a vendor, the other a
  partner. Which door opens wider?)
- Would in-shop NQR let us onboard cash-only tailors — i.e. is this a *supply acquisition* tool as
  much as a payment feature?

### 5.2 Snapnet — peer, channel, and competitor

- **Website:** https://snapnetsolutions.com (also https://snapnet.tech) · **LinkedIn:** https://ng.linkedin.com/company/snapnetnigeria
- **Offices:** Lagos and Abuja · **Leadership:** Chuma Ukeagu, CEO / Principal Consultant
- **Confidence:** High

**What they do.** Snapnet is a Nigerian enterprise technology consultancy and SaaS firm — cloud, AI,
data, security, ERP and business applications — serving financial services, oil & gas, energy,
telecoms, manufacturing and government. They position as one of Nigeria's foremost **Tier 1 Microsoft
Cloud Solution Providers**, with partners including Microsoft, Cisco, Dell, IBM, HP, Oracle and Check
Point. They also run managed services, custom software and **training**.

**Read this carefully: Snapnet is a competitor.** Cloud, data, security, software, consulting and
training — that is our service list and our Academy. They are larger, established, and enterprise-
credentialed. This does not kill the partnership, but it dictates how we play it: **do not pitch
ourselves as an alternative supplier to their clients.** Pitch complementarity, and be specific about
where we don't overlap.

**Where we genuinely complement them:**

- **Consumer-facing product work.** Their portfolio is enterprise back-office; ours is consumer
  marketplaces and mobile-first products. Different muscle.
- **Overflow and small-engagement capacity.** A consultancy their size turns away or de-prioritises
  work below a certain contract value. We take it, white-labelled, at their standard.
- **Speed.** We ship weekly. Their enterprise governance doesn't. We can be their fast-prototype arm.
- **Vertical products.** Our HR/payroll SaaS is a product they could resell into mid-market clients
  too small for a full ERP implementation.

**What we offer.**

1. **Subcontracted / white-labelled delivery capacity** — our Tech Outsourcing line exists for
   exactly this. Their client, their brand, our engineers.
2. **Our HR/payroll platform as a resellable product** for the mid-market tier under their ERP work.
3. **Academy graduates as a hiring pipeline** — they're growing and need engineers; we produce vetted
   ones with portfolio projects.
4. **A referral flow back to them:** cloud/Microsoft licensing deals our clients need and we don't
   resell.
5. **A consumer AI/cloud showcase** running on their sponsored stack — a better demo than another
   enterprise migration.

**What we ask.**

1. **Microsoft/Azure credits via their CSP relationship**, plus nomination into Microsoft partner
   or startup programmes if they can make one.
2. **Warm introductions to their enterprise clients** — for consumer/product work they don't do, and
   for **staff uniforms and branded apparel through TailorNow** (banks, oil & gas, telcos, government
   are exactly who buys uniforms at scale, and we already support group and uniform orders).
3. **A reciprocal referral agreement with defined economics** so intros are worth their while.
4. Optionally: co-bidding on tenders where our combined profile is stronger than either alone.

**Watch-outs.** Three real risks. (1) As a consultancy their instinct is to **sell us billable
hours** — steer to credits and channel. (2) We'd be **disclosing capability to a competitor**; share
what supports the partnership, not our client list or pricing. (3) On the uniform channel — be
honest internally about whether TailorNow can deliver a 500-unit corporate order today: capacity, QC,
lead time, invoicing, and 30–60 day payment terms. A fumbled first corporate order costs us the
channel and embarrasses the person who made the intro. **Verify capacity before requesting the intro.**

**Team brainstorm prompts.**
- Are we comfortable being a subcontractor to a competitor? What's the reputational upside vs the
  risk of them learning our delivery economics?
- What's TailorNow's honest maximum bulk order today, and what breaks first?
- What referral % is defensible against TailorNow's 20% commission on a large, low-margin bulk order?
- Should the Academy relationship be co-branded, or does that dilute us against a bigger brand?

### 5.3 Open Data Center — infrastructure, residency, and a resale line

> ⚠️ **Identity to confirm.** The note reads "Open data center". The strongest match in the Nigerian
> market is **Open Access Data Centres (OADC)**. Confirm from the badge or card before using this
> section, and correct the facts if it was a different company.

- **Website (assumed):** https://openaccessdc.net · Lagos: https://openaccessdc.net/lagos
- **Parent:** WIOCC · **Confidence:** Medium on identity, high on facts *if* OADC

**What they do.** OADC operates pan-African **carrier-neutral data centres**. Their flagship Nigerian
facility is in Lekki, Lagos (opened 2022) and is the **landing station for the Equiano subsea cable**
in Nigeria — a large part of why Nigerian bandwidth got faster and cheaper. Public reporting in 2025
put an announced ~$240m expansion to 24MW in Lagos on the roadmap. They serve cloud and content
providers, carriers, banks, ISPs, government and large enterprises, and market edge sites closer to
end users.

**Why this matters more at company level than it did for one product.**

1. **Data residency is close to a hard requirement for us.** Our HR/payroll platform stores employee
   PII, salary records and documents; TailorNow stores customers' body measurements. All of that is
   personal data under the NDPA. The moment we pursue a bank, government or large-enterprise client —
   which is exactly what the Snapnet channel is for — "where does the data live?" appears in the
   procurement questionnaire. Right now we don't have a strong answer.
2. **Latency and page weight.** Our consumer products are image-heavy and mobile-first, served to
   users on metered data. In-country delivery is a felt improvement, not a theoretical one.
3. **Cost.** Sponsored colocation, CDN or egress relief is a direct saving across all products.
4. **A resale/referral line.** As an IT consultancy we advise clients on infrastructure. We can put
   *their* colocation and connectivity into our recommendations — that makes us a channel, not just a
   tenant, and it's the offer that gets us taken seriously.

**What we offer.**

1. **Referral channel into our consulting client base** — infrastructure recommendations flow through
   us; we can route colo and connectivity deals their way.
2. **A growing local tenant** — sponsorship now, real spend later, across four products.
3. **An anchor local success story.** Their commercial argument is that African products should be
   hosted in Africa. A Nigerian company running consumer and enterprise SaaS in their facility proves
   it better than another enterprise migration.
4. **Consumer-brand visibility** — data centres are invisible to end users; we put their name in
   front of one.

**What we ask.**

1. **Sponsored or discounted colocation / capacity**, or entry to any startup or ecosystem programme.
2. **Edge caching / CDN for our media** so images are served in-country.
3. **A documented data-residency position** we can put in front of enterprise procurement.
4. Peering and connectivity guidance, and introductions to the ISPs, telcos and banks in the facility.

**Watch-outs.** Colocation is a heavier commitment than managed cloud and pulls us away from our
current stack — a full migration is almost certainly not worth it now. **Scope this to CDN/media and
a residency story first**, both adoptable without re-architecting. Check minimum contract terms:
discounted colo on a 24-month commitment is a liability, not a gift.

**Team brainstorm prompts.**
- Measure first: monthly image egress, and Nigerian median load time for TailorNow.
- Does data residency block any deal we're currently chasing? If yes, this jumps to high priority.
- Is there a lighter version — CDN only — that gets 80% of the benefit at 10% of the commitment?
- Is the resale angle real? Would we actually recommend them to a client, and can we say so credibly?

### 5.4 LSEG — credibility, capital, and a market-data angle

- **Website:** https://www.lseg.com
- **Confidence:** High on the company; **which division we met needs confirming** — the exchange,
  the data and analytics business, post-trade, and LSEG Technology are very different conversations.

**What they do.** London Stock Exchange Group is a 300-year-old global financial markets
infrastructure and data business operating in ~65 countries. Beyond the exchange it runs one of the
world's largest financial data and analytics businesses (Refinitiv), post-trade services, and
technology that powers exchanges worldwide — including LSEG Technology, which has supplied trading
systems to African exchanges.

**Relevant Africa activity.** LSEG has a long record in African capital markets: it hosts the
**London Africa Advisory Group**, published the **"Companies to Inspire Africa"** reports profiling
high-growth African businesses, and runs **ELITE** — a growth-company programme providing training,
mentorship, an investor network and preparation for raising capital. ELITE has expanded into Africa
(Morocco; West Africa/UEMOA via BRVM; an MoU with Nairobi Securities Exchange and FSD Africa) with
dozens of African companies on it.

**Two possible conversations — identify which before following up.**

*The credibility play (most likely).* Not commercial: **credibility, network and capital readiness**,
on a 12–36 month horizon. That isn't low value — one line about an LSEG programme changes how
investors read us, and as a two-founder company raising or winning enterprise trust, that matters.

*The data play (worth probing).* Our **Exchange** product deals in rates, settlement and market
data. If we met LSEG's data and analytics arm, there is a real product conversation about market data
access on startup or developer terms. Ask which division; don't assume.

**What we offer.**

- **Pipeline** — their programmes exist to find high-growth companies, and Nigeria's creative economy
  and SME software sector are areas they have little exposure to.
- **A financial-inclusion and creative-economy story** for their Africa agenda and sustainability
  reporting: informal artisans formalising through a digital platform, and Nigerian SMEs moving
  payroll off spreadsheets.
- **Ground-level Nigerian market intelligence** on how digital commerce and payments actually work —
  something they cannot easily buy.
- Low cost to them: a programme place and introductions, not capital or engineering.

**What we ask.**

1. **A place on ELITE** (or the current equivalent for our region) — this is the concrete, askable
   thing.
2. **Consideration for "Companies to Inspire Africa"** or equivalent visibility, if still running.
3. **Introductions into their Africa network** — the advisory group, development-finance partners
   such as FSD Africa, and investors who follow their programmes.
4. **Capital-markets readiness guidance**: governance, reporting, and what we need to look like to
   raise institutional money.
5. If it's the data arm: market and reference data access on startup terms for the Exchange.

**Watch-outs.** Do not open with an ask for capital — it reads as naïve and burns the contact. Open
with the sector insight, ask about the programme. Get the individual's actual remit; a
wrong-department follow-up is simply ignored. Also consider carefully how much we foreground the
crypto side of the Exchange with a regulated markets-infrastructure business — lead with the
software company and the products, and let them ask.

**Team brainstorm prompts.**
- Who owns this, and can they hold a capital-markets conversation credibly? (Likely Akinbayo.)
- Are we ELITE-eligible? Check current stage/revenue criteria on elite-network.com.
- What is the one insight about Nigerian digital commerce we can offer that they can't buy?
- Do we mention the Exchange at all in the first conversation?
