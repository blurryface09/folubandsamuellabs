## 2. Why FSLabs exists

Every company has a founding frustration. Ours is about **delivery**.

Nigerian businesses that need software have two bad options. They hire a large firm and become a
small account — the senior people who won the pitch disappear after the kick-off, the work goes to
whoever is available, and status meetings replace progress. Or they hire cheap and fast, and get
something that demos well, breaks under real use, was never documented, and was never secured.

Both failures have the same root cause: **the people who promised the work are not the people who
do it.**

FSLabs is built to remove that gap. Two founders, both technical enough and commercial enough to be
accountable, on every single project. When a client engages us, they get Adeseko Samuel on the
technical side and Akinbayo on strategy and delivery. Every time. Nothing is handed off to junior
contractors or offshore teams.

That is not a marketing position. It is the operating constraint we designed the company around, and
it is why our process (§7) looks the way it does.

**Say this out loud until it's natural:** *most technology firms sell you senior people and deliver
junior ones. We only have senior people, and they're the ones on your project.*

## 6. Values

Four values. They are not decoration — they are how work gets judged here. Each one below has a
"looks like" and a "does not mean", because a value you can't apply on a Tuesday afternoon isn't a
value.

### 01 · Innovation
*We don't follow trends — we set them. Every solution we build pushes the boundary of what's possible.*

- **Looks like:** proposing the approach nobody asked for because it's genuinely better; using new
  tooling where it earns its place; building our own products instead of only billing hours.
- **Does not mean:** novelty for its own sake, or putting a client's production system on something
  unproven to satisfy our curiosity.

### 02 · Ambition
*We think big, move fast, and refuse to settle. Small thinking has no place here.*

- **Looks like:** going after the enterprise client rather than the safe small one; shipping weekly;
  treating "that's how it's done in Nigeria" as a challenge rather than a limit.
- **Does not mean:** over-committing on timelines. Ambition is about what we aim at, not what we
  promise a client on a call.

### 03 · Integrity
*We say what we mean and deliver what we promise. No fluff, no shortcuts, no excuses.*

- **Looks like:** telling a client a date will slip the moment you know, not the week it's due;
  scoping honestly even when it loses the deal; admitting a mistake plainly and fixing it.
- **Does not mean:** bluntness as a personality. Integrity is about accuracy, not tone.

### 04 · Impact
*Technology is just a tool. What matters is what it changes — for our clients and for the world.*

- **Looks like:** measuring the outcome, not the output; asking what a feature is actually for;
  choosing the boring solution that solves the problem over the impressive one that doesn't.
- **Does not mean:** grand claims. Impact is specific and usually small at first.

## 7. How we work — the process

Four stages, every engagement, no exceptions.

| | Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **01** | **Brief** | The client tells us what they need. We listen, ask the right questions, and understand the real problem **before touching a keyboard**. |
| **02** | **Scope** | We define the work clearly. Realistic timelines. Honest deliverables. No scope creep, no surprises. |
| **03** | **Build** | We do the work. Software gets built, systems get secured, solutions get shipped. **No status theatre.** |
| **04** | **Deliver** | The client receives something that works, is documented, and is ready for the real world. |

### What this means day to day

- **Understand before building.** Questions early are cheap; rework is not.
- **Scope honestly.** If a timeline isn't realistic, say so during Scope, not during Build.
- **No status theatre.** Report real progress, including problems. Bad news early is a service; bad
  news late is a failure.
- **Document as you go.** Documentation is part of delivery, not an optional extra.
- **Secure from day one.** Not a phase at the end.
- **Respond within 24 hours.** That is the promise on our contact form. Honour it.

## 8. Three business lines, one engine

FSLabs has three revenue lines. Understanding how they feed each other is the most important
strategic idea in this document — it is why we are not simply an agency.

```
   SERVICES  ──funds──▶  PRODUCTS  ──prove capability──▶  SERVICES
      ▲                                                      │
      │                                                      │
   staffs                                                 creates
      │                                                   demand for
      └────────────  ACADEMY  ◀──────── credibility ──────────┘
```

- **Services fund the company.** Client revenue pays for everything else. This is the engine.
- **Products prove we can do it.** An agency says it can build a marketplace. We point at TailorNow.
  Products are our portfolio, our R&D, and eventually our recurring revenue.
- **The Academy supplies the talent** that lets services scale past two founders — and produces
  alumni who become clients, referrers and hires.
- **Each one makes the others more credible.** A security firm that teaches ethical hacking is more
  believable. A training company whose instructors ship production systems is more believable. A
  product company that also consults understands business, not just code.

**The strategic risk to name out loud:** services revenue is the only one funding the others, and
services capacity is capped by two founders. Everything in Part III is ultimately about relieving
that constraint.

## 12. Who we serve

Four audiences. They want different things and need to be spoken to differently — this section is
especially for the content strategist.

### A · The mid-to-large Nigerian enterprise
*Banks, fintechs, oil & gas, telecoms, manufacturing, government agencies.*
- **They care about:** reliability, security, compliance, someone senior being accountable, not
  being embarrassed by a vendor.
- **Their fear:** picking a small firm and getting let down.
- **Lead with:** the two-founder accountability model, security credentials, our own products as
  evidence, the documented-delivery standard.
- **Buys:** cybersecurity assessments, custom software, consulting, outsourced capacity.

### B · The growing Nigerian SME
*20–200 staff, digitising for the first time — moving off spreadsheets and WhatsApp.*
- **They care about:** cost, speed, someone who explains things without condescension.
- **Their fear:** paying for something they don't understand and can't maintain.
- **Lead with:** plain language, fixed honest scope, the HR & Payroll platform as an off-the-shelf
  start, our "no status theatre" transparency.
- **Buys:** the HR platform subscription, small builds, cloud setup.

### C · The Academy student
*Career-changers and early-career developers who want a job, not a certificate.*
- **They care about:** will this actually get me hired.
- **Their fear:** paying for a bootcamp that teaches theory and abandons them.
- **Lead with:** taught by engineers who ship, 3+ portfolio projects you own, placement referrals,
  the alumni network.
- **Buys:** a track, and later refers friends.

### D · The partner
*Payment providers, cloud and infrastructure providers, larger consultancies, investors.*
- **They care about:** what we bring them — volume, reach, capability, a category they can't reach.
- **Lead with:** offer before ask. See the partnership brief in
  `docs/partnerships/summit-partnership-brief.md`.

### Who we are not for

Saying this clearly protects the brand and saves everyone's time:

- Anyone shopping purely on lowest price. We are not the cheapest and should never pretend to be.
- Anyone who wants a body-shop that takes instructions without asking why.
- Anyone who wants work done in a way we consider insecure or dishonest.

**Ten seconds** — when someone asks what you do:
> FSLabs is a Nigerian technology and cybersecurity company. We build software, secure systems, train

**Thirty seconds** — the version with the hook:
> FSLabs is a technology and cybersecurity company in Lagos. We do software development, security,

**Two minutes** — the version that wins a meeting:
> Most technology firms sell you senior people and deliver junior ones. The people who won the pitch
> disappear after kick-off, and the work goes to whoever is free. That gap is where projects die.
>
> FSLabs is built to remove it. We're a two-founder company — Samuel on the technical side,

>
> And we don't just consult. We build and run our own products — a fashion marketplace with payments

> can point at it. We also run an academy training engineers, which is how we grow without lowering

## 18. How to defend the company

You will get these objections. Have the answer ready. Never bluff — every answer below is true.

**"You're only two people. Can you actually handle our project?"**
> That's exactly why the work gets done properly. You're not a small account to us, and you're never
> handed to someone junior. Where we need extra capacity we bring in vetted people — often our own
> Academy graduates — but the founders stay accountable for delivery. If a project is genuinely
> beyond what we can do well, we'll tell you during Scope rather than take your money.

**"You're new. What's your track record?"**
> Look at what we run ourselves. TailorNow is a live marketplace handling payments, split settlement
> to vendors, and delivery logistics. We built an HR and payroll platform holding real employee and
> salary data. And an exchange processing requests in about thirty minutes. Most firms our size have
> a portfolio of other people's brochure sites. We have production systems we operate ourselves.

**"Why you instead of a big firm?"**
> With a big firm you get their process and their B-team. With us you get both founders and no
> layers. We're faster, we're accountable, and we'll tell you no. If you need a thousand-person
> global rollout, use them. If you need something built properly by people who answer the phone, use
> us.

**"Why should we trust a Nigerian firm with this?"**
> Because we're held to the same standard as anyone else and we've chosen to be. Security from day
> one, documented delivery, honest timelines. Being local is an advantage — we understand Nigerian
> payments, Nigerian regulation and Nigerian users in a way an offshore team doesn't. And we're in
> the same time zone as your business.

**"A freelancer quoted me a fraction of that."**
> They probably will build something. The question is what happens in month six — when it needs to
> scale, when it gets attacked, when the freelancer has moved on and there's no documentation. We're
> not the cheapest and we don't try to be. We're the option where it still works next year.

**"Is our data safe with you?"**
> Security is our founder's own discipline, not a subcontracted extra — we do penetration testing and
> security audits as a service line. We build secure from day one rather than bolting it on at the
> end. And what we find on your systems never leaves FSLabs.

**"What if something happens to one of you?"**
> Fair question, and it's why the Academy and our vetted bench exist — we're deliberately building
> capacity beyond the two of us. Everything we deliver is documented, so nothing lives only in
> someone's head. That's part of why documentation is in our definition of finished.

**"Can you deliver on time?"**
> We scope timelines we believe, which sometimes means quoting longer than you'd like. If something is
> going to slip, you hear it the moment we know — not the week it's due. Ask us for a date we can't
> hit and we'll say so.
