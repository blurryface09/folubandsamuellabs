## How to use this document

This handbook exists to make three specific things possible.

**1. Onboarding.** A new team member should be able to read this and understand what FSLabs is, why it
exists, how it earns, how work gets judged, and what is expected of them, without a founder having to
explain it verbally.

**2. Representing the company.** Every one of us will at some point be asked "so what does FSLabs
actually do?" or "why should we use you instead of a bigger firm?" Part IV gives you the answers. You
should be able to defend this company in a room with no founder present.

**3. Content and commercial planning.** Our content strategist needs to know what we stand for, who we
are talking to, what we sound like, and what we are pushing on, before they can build a plan. Parts
III and IV are written for that.

**Two companion documents.** The **Staff Policy** (`STAFF_POLICY.md`) covers hours, leave, pay, client
data rules, ownership of work, and what to do when something goes wrong. Read it alongside this one.
The **partnership brief** covers the organisations we met at the summit.

**On the priorities in Part III.** They state what we are pushing on and who owns each one, without
target figures. The direction is settled. The numbers attached to each priority are reviewed by the
founders quarterly and communicated separately, so this handbook stays accurate as those targets move.

## 2. Why FSLabs exists

Every company has a founding frustration. Ours is about delivery.

Nigerian businesses that need software have two bad options. They hire a large firm and become a small
account, where the senior people who won the pitch disappear after the kick off meeting, the work goes
to whoever is available, and status meetings replace progress. Or they hire cheap and fast, and get
something that demos well, breaks under real use, was never documented, and was never secured.

Both failures have the same root cause: **the people who promised the work are not the people who do
it.**

FSLabs is built to remove that gap. Two founders, each technical enough and commercial enough to be
accountable, on every single project. When a client engages us they get Adeseko Samuel on the
technical side and Akinbayo on strategy and delivery. Every time. Nothing is handed off to junior
contractors or offshore teams.

That is not a marketing position. It is the operating constraint we designed the company around, and
it is why our process in section 7 looks the way it does.

**Say this until it is natural:** most technology firms sell you senior people and deliver junior ones.
We only have senior people, and they are the ones on your project.

## 5. The standard

Everything we release has to **work, be documented, and be ready for the real world.**

If a piece of work fails any one of those three it is not finished, regardless of the deadline. This
is the sentence to fall back on whenever you are unsure whether something is ready to hand over.

Samuel's version: everything we release has to actually work, scale, and be secure from day one.
Security is not a phase at the end.

## 6. Values

Four values. They are not decoration. They are how work gets judged here. Each has a "looks like" and
a "does not mean", because a value you cannot apply on a Tuesday afternoon is not a value.

### 01 Innovation
*We don't follow trends. We set them. Every solution we build pushes the boundary of what is possible.*

- **Looks like:** proposing the approach nobody asked for because it is genuinely better. Using new
  tooling where it earns its place. Building our own products instead of only billing hours.
- **Does not mean:** novelty for its own sake, or putting a client's production system on something
  unproven to satisfy our curiosity.

### 02 Ambition
*We think big, move fast, and refuse to settle. Small thinking has no place here.*

- **Looks like:** going after the enterprise client rather than the safe small one. Shipping weekly.
  Treating "that is how it is done in Nigeria" as a challenge rather than a limit.
- **Does not mean:** over committing on timelines. Ambition is about what we aim at, not what we
  promise a client on a call.

### 03 Integrity
*We say what we mean and deliver what we promise. No fluff, no shortcuts, no excuses.*

- **Looks like:** telling a client a date will slip the moment you know, not the week it is due.
  Scoping honestly even when it loses the deal. Admitting a mistake plainly and fixing it.
- **Does not mean:** bluntness as a personality. Integrity is about accuracy, not tone.

### 04 Impact
*Technology is just a tool. What matters is what it changes, for our clients and for the world.*

- **Looks like:** measuring the outcome rather than the output. Asking what a feature is actually for.
  Choosing the boring solution that solves the problem over the impressive one that does not.
- **Does not mean:** grand claims. Impact is specific, and usually small at first.

## 7. How we work: the process

Four stages, every engagement, no exceptions.

| | Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **01** | **Brief** | The client tells us what they need. We listen, ask the right questions, and understand the real problem **before touching a keyboard**. |
| **02** | **Scope** | We define the work clearly. Realistic timelines. Honest deliverables. No scope creep, no surprises. |
| **03** | **Build** | We do the work. Software gets built, systems get secured, solutions get shipped. **No status theatre.** |
| **04** | **Deliver** | The client receives something that works, is documented, and is ready for the real world. |

### What this means daily

- **Understand before building.** Questions early are cheap. Rework is not.
- **Scope honestly.** If a timeline is not realistic, say so during Scope, not during Build.
- **No status theatre.** Report real progress, including problems. Bad news early is a service. Bad
  news late is a failure.
- **Document as you go.** Documentation is part of delivery, not an optional extra.
- **Secure from day one.** Not a phase at the end.
- **Respond within 24 hours.** That is the promise on our contact form. Honour it.

## 8. Three business lines, one engine

FSLabs has three revenue lines. Understanding how they feed each other is the most important
strategic idea in this document. It is why we are not simply an agency.

> **Services** fund the company → **Products** prove we can build → back into **Services** credibility
>
> **Academy** staffs Services, and Services credibility fills the Academy

- **Services will fund the company.** Client revenue is what pays for everything else. This is the
  engine we are building, and today it is running on small jobs rather than full engagements.
- **Products prove we can do it.** An agency says it can build a marketplace. We point at TailorNow.
  Right now this is our strongest asset by far, because it is the one claim we can fully back.
- **The Academy supplies the talent** that lets services grow past two founders, and produces alumni
  who become hires, referrers and clients.
- **Each one makes the others believable.** A security firm that teaches ethical hacking is more
  credible. A training company whose instructors ship production systems is more credible. A product
  company that also consults understands business, not just code.

**Two risks, said out loud.** Services capacity is capped by two founders, so growth is constrained
before it starts. And services is not yet funding anything, because we have not landed a full
engagement. The first of those is a long problem. The second is the immediate one, and it is why the
services priority in Part III is written the way it is.

## 12. Who we serve

Four audiences. They want different things and need to be spoken to differently. This section is
especially for the content strategist.

### A. The medium to large Nigerian enterprise
*Banks, fintechs, oil and gas, telecoms, manufacturing, government agencies.*
- **They care about:** reliability, security, compliance, someone senior being accountable, not being
  embarrassed by a vendor.
- **Their fear:** picking a small firm and getting let down.
- **Lead with:** the accountability of both founders on the work, security credentials, our own
  products as evidence, and the documented delivery standard.
- **Buys:** cybersecurity assessments, custom software, consulting, outsourced capacity.

### B. The growing Nigerian SME
*Twenty to two hundred staff, digitising for the first time, moving off spreadsheets and WhatsApp.*
- **They care about:** cost, speed, someone who explains things without condescension.
- **Their fear:** paying for something they do not understand and cannot maintain.
- **Lead with:** plain language, fixed honest scope, and our refusal to do status theatre. The HR and
  Payroll platform becomes the natural opening offer once its review pass is done.
- **Buys:** the HR platform subscription, small builds, cloud setup.

### C. The Academy student
*Career changers and new developers who want a job, not a certificate.*
- **They care about:** whether this actually gets them hired.
- **Their fear:** paying for a bootcamp that teaches theory and then abandons them.
- **Lead with:** taught by engineers who ship, three or more portfolio projects you own, placement
  referrals, and the alumni network.
- **Buys:** a track, and then refers friends.

### D. The partner
*Payment providers, cloud and infrastructure providers, larger consultancies, investors.*
- **They care about:** what we bring them. Volume, reach, capability, a category they cannot reach.
- **Lead with:** the offer before the ask. See `docs/partnerships/summit-partnership-brief.md`.

### Who we are not for

Saying this clearly protects the brand and saves everyone's time.

- Anyone shopping purely on lowest price. We are not the cheapest and should never pretend to be.
- Anyone who wants a body shop that takes instructions without asking why.
- Anyone who wants work done in a way we consider insecure or dishonest.

# PART III: WHERE WE ARE GOING

## 13. How we set priorities

One priority per business line, plus one for the company. Four in total, not twelve, so that everyone
can remember them.

Each has a direction and a named owner. Target figures sit with the founders and are reviewed
quarterly, so that this handbook does not go stale every time a number moves.

## 14. Our four priorities

### Company: break the two founder ceiling
**Owner: both founders.**

Services capacity is capped by two people, and every other ambition depends on that changing. The work
is to build capacity we can put in front of a client, made of Academy alumni, contractors and hires,
without dropping the standard in section 5. Founder accountability on every project stays exactly as
promised, even once the founders are not the ones typing.

### Services: land the first real engagements, then build toward recurring revenue
**Owner: Akinbayo.** **Priority lines: Cybersecurity and Tech Outsourcing.**

The immediate job is the first full paid engagement. Not a small job, a real one, delivered to the
standard in section 5 and finished well enough that the client agrees to be named. That single outcome
unlocks more than any other thing on this list, because it converts our capability story into a
reference.

The direction after that is to move the shape of our revenue from project by project toward retainers
and monthly contracts. These two lines are where that is possible: security testing repeats on a cycle,
and outsourced capacity bills monthly. They also reinforce each other, because a security relationship
is how you earn the trust that leads to the engineering contract.

Cybersecurity is the priority because it is Samuel's own discipline, which means the skill is real even
though the engagement history is not yet. Getting the first security engagement, even small and keenly
priced, is worth more than the fee.

### Academy: cohorts that produce staffable engineers
**Owner: to be assigned by the founders.**

Grow the cohorts, and treat **placement rather than enrolment** as the measure that matters. Enrolment
is revenue. Placement is the promise. The Academy priority meets the company priority at exactly one
point: alumni good enough to put on client work. That is the outcome to optimise for.

### Products: from proof to paying
**Owner: Samuel.**

Get one product past the point of being a portfolio piece and into paying for itself. The HR and
Payroll platform is the candidate, because subscription revenue is the only kind that compounds
without selling more hours. TailorNow should be measured on one honest number rather than several
flattering ones. FSLabs Exchange needs an explicit decision on whether it is a growth line or a
maintained service, because ambiguity there costs attention.

## 15. The metrics that matter

Track few things, honestly.

| Line | The one number | Why this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Services | Recurring revenue as a share of monthly cost | Tells you whether the company survives a slow month |
| Services | Proposal to close rate | Tells you whether the pitch or the pricing is the problem |
| Products | Paying subscriptions on the HR platform | The only product number that is not vanity |
| TailorNow | Completed orders per month | Captures supply, demand and trust in one figure |
| Academy | Graduate placement rate | The promise we make, so measure the promise |
| Delivery | On time delivery against the scoped date | Integrity made measurable |
| Brand | Inbound enquiries per month | Whether the content plan is working |

## 16. What we are deliberately not doing yet

Naming this prevents drift, and tells the content strategist what not to promise.

- Not expanding outside Nigeria until the delivery constraint in section 14 is relieved.
- Not adding a seventh service line. Six is already wide for our size.
- Not competing on price.
- Not building new products until an existing one pays for itself.

## 18. How to defend the company

You will get these objections. Have the answer ready. Never bluff, because every answer below is true.

**"You are only two people. Can you actually handle our project?"**
> That is exactly why the work gets done properly. You are not a small account to us, and you are never
> handed to someone junior. Where we need extra capacity we bring in vetted people, often our own
> Academy graduates, but the founders stay accountable for delivery. If a project is genuinely beyond
> what we can do well, we will tell you during Scope rather than take your money.

**"You are new. What is your track record?"**
> We are new, and I would rather tell you that than dress it up. What we can show you is what we have
> built ourselves. TailorNow is a working marketplace that handles payments, split settlement out to
> vendors, and delivery logistics. We built an HR and payroll platform that holds employee and salary
> records. We run an exchange that processes requests in about thirty minutes. And we are building
> UrbanPulse, a citizen reporting and transport intelligence platform. Most firms at our stage show you
> other people's brochure sites. We can show you systems we designed, built and operate. What we do not
> have yet is a long client list, and if that is what you need, I will say so rather than waste your
> time.

**"Have you done this for a client before?"**
> Not at this scale, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. We have done smaller paid pieces of work,
> and we have built and run our own products, which is where the capability comes from. What you get
> from working with us early is both founders on your project and a level of attention you will not get
> from a firm where you are one account of forty. What you should ask for in return is a scope small
> enough to test us on. Give us a first piece of work, hold us to the standard, and decide from there.

**"Why you instead of a big firm?"**
> With a big firm you get their process and their second team. With us you get both founders and no
> layers. We are faster, we are accountable, and we will tell you no. If you need a thousand person
> global rollout, use them. If you need something built properly by people who answer the phone, use
> us.

**"Why should we trust a Nigerian firm with this?"**
> Because we are held to the same standard as anyone else, and we have chosen to be. Security from day
> one, documented delivery, honest timelines. Being local is an advantage: we understand Nigerian
> payments, Nigerian regulation and Nigerian users in a way an offshore team does not. And we are in
> the same time zone as your business.

**"A freelancer quoted me a fraction of that."**
> They probably will build something. The question is what happens in month six, when it needs to
> scale, when it gets attacked, and when the freelancer has moved on and left no documentation. We are
> not the cheapest and we do not try to be. We are the option where it still works next year.

**"Is our data safe with you?"**
> Security is our founder's own discipline, not something we subcontract. Samuel is trained and
> practised in it, and it is why penetration testing and security audits are part of what we offer. We
> build secure from day one rather than bolting it on at the end. We have a written rule that client
> code and data never goes into any third party AI tool. And what we find on your systems never leaves
> FSLabs.

**"Have you actually done a security engagement?"**
> Not a paid one under FSLabs, no. The skill is real and it is Samuel's discipline, but I am not going
> to claim an engagement history we do not have. If you want to test us on it, scope something contained
> and price it accordingly. You will get our founder doing the work personally, and a report you can
> hand to your board.

**"What if something happens to one of you?"**
> Fair question, and it is why the Academy and our vetted bench exist. We are deliberately building
> capacity beyond the two of us. Everything we deliver is documented, so nothing lives only in
> someone's head. That is part of why documentation is in our definition of finished.

**"Can you deliver on time?"**
> We scope timelines we believe, which sometimes means quoting longer than you would like. If something
> is going to slip you hear it the moment we know, not the week it is due. Ask us for a date we cannot
> hit and we will say so.

### If you do not know the answer

Say so, and say what happens next. *"I do not want to guess at that. Let me get you a proper answer
from Samuel today."* That is a stronger answer than a confident wrong one, and it is what Integrity
means in practice.
