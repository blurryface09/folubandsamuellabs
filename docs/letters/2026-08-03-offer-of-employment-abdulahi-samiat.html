After reviewing your application and talking it through with you, we are pleased to offer you employment with Folub & Samuel Labs Limited (“FSLabs” or “the Company”) as our Content Strategist, on the terms set out below.

Your record made the decision easy. Growing an audience from 137 to 14,700 followers in sixty days, and going from four views to over 1.1 million in the same window, is not luck. That is strategy, held steady week after week. Building a content function from nothing at Carebrij tells us you can also do the harder thing, which is taking a complicated idea and turning it into something people actually want to read. We build technology and security products. Our problem has never been the engineering. It is that too few people understand what we have built. That is the problem we want you to solve.

2. Purpose of the Role

The Content Strategist owns how FSLabs sounds and what FSLabs publishes. You will set the editorial direction for all of our brands and products, which today means the FSLabs corporate brand, FS Exchange, and FSLabs Training. Your job is to take our work in software engineering and security and turn it into writing that is clear, credible, built for the reader, and good enough to bring in real enquiries. You are not here to post on a schedule someone else set. You are the person who decides what story we tell, who we tell it to, and why it will land.

3. Key Responsibilities and Job Roles

3.1  Content Strategy and Planning

  • Own the Company’s content strategy across every brand, product, and channel, and review it each quarter against what the business is trying to achieve.
  • Decide who each brand is talking to and what it talks about, then build and run the monthly content calendar.
  • Audit the content regularly. Retire whatever is not working and move that effort to what is.
  • Advise leadership on positioning, messaging, and the order in which we launch new products and services.

3.2  Writing, Editorial, and Brand Voice

  • Research, write, and edit across every format we use: articles, explainers, case studies, newsletters, copy for landing pages and products, scripts, and campaign copy.
  • Take technical material, whether a security incident, an engineering decision, or how a product actually works, and turn it into writing anyone can follow without losing accuracy.
  • Interview our engineers, our security staff, and our clients so the work draws on original material rather than recycled industry talk. Check every claim you publish, and clear technical statements with the Chief Technology Officer before release.
  • Write and maintain the FSLabs brand voice and style guide, and act as the last set of eyes on anything that goes out under a Company brand.

3.3  Social Media and Community Growth

  • Plan, produce, schedule, and publish across our social platforms, currently LinkedIn, Instagram, X, and TikTok, holding a steady rhythm through the quiet stretches as well as the launches.
  • Grow reach, engagement, and a following worth having, through formats you have tested rather than sheer volume.
  • Handle community interaction, passing commercial leads to the Business Lead and anything security sensitive to the Chief Technology Officer.

3.4  SEO and Organic Discoverability

  • Research the keywords and the questions our prospective clients actually type, and keep a rolling roadmap of content that answers them.
  • Improve what sits on each page, including titles, meta descriptions, headings, and internal links, working with the technical team on page structure and metadata.

3.5  Campaigns and Commercial Support

  • Build campaigns around content and tie them to outcomes we can count: enquiries, waitlist signups, training enrolments, and platform registrations.
  • Write and maintain the email and newsletter sequences for onboarding, nurture, and announcements.
  • Support FSLabs Training and FS Exchange, and write the material our sales and partnership conversations need.

3.6  Measurement, Collaboration, and Governance

  • Decide which numbers matter for each brand and channel, set the baselines in your first month, and report to the Chief Technology Officer every month with a recommendation attached, not just the figures.
  • Test formats, hooks, and timing properly, and feed what you learn back into the strategy.
  • Work with engineering, security, design, and the business side to get your facts right and keep the messaging aligned. Brief any freelancer we bring in to help.
  • Treat client and system information as confidential unless told otherwise, and keep the content records tidy and accessible, since they belong to the Company.

4. Deliverables in the First Ninety Days

By day 60A content calendar live and running across all active brands, a publishing rhythm we can rely on, and your first monthly performance report delivered.
