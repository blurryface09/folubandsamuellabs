Offer of Employment: Lead Security Engineer

Dear Ikenna,

We are pleased to offer you employment with Folub & Samuel Labs Limited (“FSLabs” or “the Company”) as Lead Security Engineer, on the terms set out below.

You are the most experienced security practitioner we have met through this process, and that is why the word lead is in the title. Security is not a side service here. It is half of what this company sells and the standard we are judged by, so the person holding the technical line on it matters more than almost any other hire we will make this year.

Because titles are easy to misread, we want to be plain about what lead means at FSLabs today, and Clause 1 and section 4 of this letter set it out. You lead on expertise. You set the technical standard for security work, other security work is reviewed by you, and your judgement carries real weight in what we do and how we do it. You are not being handed budget, hiring authority, or a veto over the business. Decisions that commit the Company sit with the Chief Technology Officer. Your job is to make sure those decisions are made with the facts in front of them, and to say so clearly, in writing, when you think we are about to get something wrong.

2. Purpose of the Role

You own the technical quality of security at FSLabs, on two fronts that reinforce each other. Outward, you carry the security work we sell to clients, from assessment through to the report that lands on their desk, and the credibility of that work is the credibility of the firm. Inward, you are responsible for FSLabs not being the embarrassing case study: our own systems, access, and practices held to the standard we charge others to reach. A security firm that gets breached does not recover, and we would rather pay someone good to prevent that than explain it afterwards.

3. Key Responsibilities and Job Roles

3.1  Client Security Delivery

  • Scope, plan, and run the security engagements we sell, including assessments, penetration tests, configuration reviews, and audits.
  • Write the client deliverable yourself or review it before it leaves: findings, severity, evidence, business impact, and remediation a client can actually act on.
  • Present and defend findings to client technical teams and to their management, adjusting the language without softening the substance.
  • Confirm that every engagement has written authorisation and an agreed scope before any testing begins, and refuse to proceed until it does.
  • Support Akinbayo on the technical content of proposals and client security questions during the sales conversation, so we do not promise work we cannot deliver.

3.2  Offensive Security and Research

  • Conduct penetration testing and adversarial assessment across web, network, cloud, and application layers as engagements require.
  • Keep our testing methodology current, documented, and repeatable, so results do not depend on who ran the test.
  • Build and maintain internal tooling and a safe laboratory environment for research, kept isolated from production and from client systems.
  • Track emerging vulnerabilities and techniques relevant to our clients and our own stack, and tell us when something changes what we should be doing.
  • Handle exploit code, proof of concept material, and vulnerability detail as sensitive by default, stored and shared under controls you define and we agree.

3.3  Internal Security and Infrastructure

  • Own the security posture of our own systems: identity and access, hardening, network and cloud configuration, patching discipline, and secrets management.
  • Establish and run logging, monitoring, and alerting sufficient to notice a problem before a client or an attacker tells us about it.
  • Write and rehearse our incident response plan, and lead the technical response when something happens, reporting to the Chief Technology Officer throughout.
  • Review our platforms, including FS Exchange and the systems behind FSLabs Training, and escalate what you find with a clear severity and a recommendation.
  • Set the access standards our junior staff work to, and review their access requests. Our Junior Cloud Engineer will hold credentials to real environments, and how that is governed is yours to specify.

3.4  Governance, Risk and Compliance

  • Write and maintain the Company security policies: acceptable use, devices, access control, data handling, incident response, and vendor review.
  • Establish what the Nigeria Data Protection Act requires of us in practice, and keep us on the right side of it as we take on client data and third party data arrangements.
  • Lay the groundwork for a recognised security standard such as ISO 27001, at a pace the business can carry, so that enterprise clients have something to point at.
  • Answer client security questionnaires and due diligence requests accurately. Never claim a control we do not have.
  • Assess the security and data protection implications of new partnerships and vendors before we sign, including any arrangement that puts personal data through our systems.

3.5  Technical Leadership

  • Set the security standard others work to, and review security work produced by anyone else at FSLabs.
  • Mentor junior staff on security practice, and build the habits early: least privilege, no secrets in repositories, and reporting your own mistakes without fear.
  • Help us hire the security people who come after you, including assessing candidates technically.
  • Give a straight recommendation, in writing, when you believe a decision is wrong. Being overruled sometimes is part of the job. Staying quiet is not.

4. Escalation and Decision Rights

So that this is never in doubt, the following is how security decisions are made while the Company is this size. You identify and assess risk, and you recommend. Where you judge that shipping, configuring, or accepting something creates serious risk, you say so in writing to the Chief Technology Officer, with your reasoning and your recommendation. He decides, and the decision and its reasons are recorded. If he decides against your recommendation, that record protects both of you. This arrangement is deliberate for now and will be revisited as the security function grows.

6. General Terms and Conditions

  1. Authorised testing only. You will conduct security testing only against systems the Company is authorised in writing to test, and only within the agreed scope, timing, and method. You will not test, probe, scan, or attempt access against any system outside that scope, whether belonging to a client, a prospect, a partner, or a third party, and whether or not you believe it harmless. Unauthorised access to computer systems is a criminal offence in Nigeria under the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention etc.) Act, and no instruction from anyone at FSLabs, including the Chief Technology Officer, overrides this clause. If you are ever asked to cross that line, refuse and put the refusal in writing.
  2. Handling of findings and tooling. Vulnerability details, exploit code, proof of concept material, client evidence, and testing output remain the property of the Company and must be stored, shared, and destroyed under the controls we agree. None of it leaves Company systems, and none of it is retained personally when you leave.
  3. Responsible disclosure. Where you discover a vulnerability outside a client engagement, including in third party or open source software, you will tell the Chief Technology Officer before disclosing anything publicly, and we will agree the disclosure approach together. We support responsible disclosure and will not sit on your findings, but we will not be surprised by them either.
  4. Confidentiality. You will hold the deepest access in this company and will see what is not public about our clients, their systems, their weaknesses, and our own. You agree to keep all of it strictly confidential during your employment and after it ends. You will sign the Company’s standard nondisclosure agreement on or before your start date, and client engagements may require you to sign theirs.
  5. Intellectual property. Everything you create in the course of your employment, including tooling, scripts, methodology, policies, and reports, belongs to the Company along with the intellectual property rights in it. Where you build something of general value and wish to release it as open source, ask, and we will consider it on its merits.
  6. Outside engagements. You may continue independent security research, bug bounty work, competitive events, teaching, and writing in your own time. All we ask is that you disclose it in writing, that it does not compete with us, that it uses none of our confidential information, client data, tooling, or infrastructure, and that anything you find in the course of it stays separate from your work here. Paid security work for another firm requires prior written consent.
  7. Notice. Either party may end this employment on one (1) month’s written notice, or payment in lieu of it. On departure you will hand over credentials, tooling, documentation, and any work in progress in an orderly way. Nothing here limits the Company’s right to dismiss summarily for gross misconduct, and given the access this role carries, a serious breach of Clause 1 or Clause 2 would qualify.
  8. Policies. This letter governs your employment, along with the Company handbook and the policies we issue from time to time, including the ones you will be writing.
  9. Documentation. This offer is conditional upon you providing, on or before your start date, valid identification, evidence of your stated qualifications and certifications, and the details of two referees. Given the access this role carries, we may also ask for a background check, and we will tell you before we do.
  10. Governing law. This offer, and any contract of employment that follows from it, is governed by the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, including the Labour Act, the Nigeria Data Protection Act, and the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention etc.) Act.
  11. Entire agreement. This letter is the whole of what we have agreed about your employment, and it replaces everything discussed or represented before it.

Acceptance. Please confirm by signing and returning a copy of this letter on or before 10 August 2026. If we do not hear from you by then, the offer lapses without further notice. If anything in here reads wrong to you, and particularly if the description of what lead means does not match the conversation you thought you were having, raise it with me before you sign rather than after.

Ikenna, we are hiring you because you know this work better than we do, and we would rather be told when we are wrong than flattered. Say the hard thing early and you will find it lands well here.
