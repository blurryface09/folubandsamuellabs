Offer of Employment: Junior Cloud Engineer (Trainee)

Dear Oyinkansola,

After reviewing your application, we are pleased to offer you employment with Folub & Samuel Labs Limited (“FSLabs” or “the Company”) as a Junior Cloud Engineer (Trainee), on the terms set out below.

We will be straight with you about why. You are not coming to us with years of cloud experience, and we are not pretending otherwise. What you are bringing is a science degree that trained you to be careful, a year in a hospital laboratory where being careless has consequences, and clear evidence that you have started teaching yourself this field on your own time. People who move from biochemistry into engineering because they want to, rather than because someone told them to, tend to learn quickly. That is what we are hiring.

So read the title honestly. Trainee means we expect you to arrive knowing very little, and we expect that to change fast. You will be given real work, with someone checking it, and the amount of checking will drop as you earn it.

2. Purpose of the Role

You are here to become an engineer we can rely on, and to be useful while you get there. In practice that means two things running at once. You will carry a share of the real work that keeps our infrastructure and internal systems running, starting with the parts that are safe to hand a beginner and widening as your judgement grows. Alongside that, you will follow a structured learning path towards a recognised cloud certification, with time set aside for it rather than squeezed into evenings.

3. Key Responsibilities and Job Roles

3.1  Cloud Infrastructure and Operations

  • Assist in provisioning and configuring cloud resources for our platforms, following the patterns and the review process the Chief Technology Officer sets.
  • Monitor systems, watch the dashboards and logs, and raise anything that looks wrong early rather than waiting to be sure.
  • Help with routine maintenance: applying updates, rotating credentials on schedule, checking that backups actually restore.
  • Never change production on your own initiative. Propose it, get it reviewed, then do it.

3.2  Automation and Version Control

  • Use Git and GitHub properly for everything you write. Small commits, clear messages, work reviewed before it merges.
  • Build and maintain GitHub Actions workflows for testing, building, and deployment, extending what you already practised in the Datacom simulation into work that ships.
  • Automate the small repetitive tasks you meet. If you did it by hand three times, write the script.
  • Keep secrets, keys, and credentials out of repositories without exception.

3.3  Internal Systems and Technical Support

  • Handle first line support for the team: accounts, access requests, devices, and the everyday problems that stop people working.
  • Maintain the internal tooling and the asset and access records, so we always know who has access to what.
  • Onboard and offboard accounts promptly and completely, since an account nobody closed is how firms get breached.

3.4  Security Practice

  • Follow the principle of least privilege in everything you touch. Ask for the access the task needs and no more.
  • Apply our policies on devices, acceptable use, and two step verification to yourself first.
  • Report mistakes immediately, including your own. We would far rather hear about a misconfiguration from you than find it later. Nobody here is punished for reporting an error honestly.
  • Treat every client system and every piece of client data as confidential.

3.5  Documentation and Reporting

  • Write down what you do as you do it: configurations, procedures, and the fixes that worked, in language the next person can follow.
  • Keep a running log of your work and your open questions, and bring it to the weekly review.
  • When something breaks, record what happened and what stopped it happening again.

3.6  Learning and Development

  • Work through the learning path agreed with the Chief Technology Officer towards a recognised cloud certification, and keep to the milestones in it.
  • Use the FSLabs Training material available to you, and ask for what is missing.
  • Ask questions early and often. A trainee who asks is doing the job. A trainee who guesses quietly and breaks something is not.

4. What We Expect in the First Ninety Days

By day 30Fully set up and working securely on our systems. Comfortable with our Git workflow. Handling routine support requests on your own. Your learning path agreed and started.
By day 60Provisioning and configuring basic cloud resources under review. One useful automation of your own, built and merged. Your documentation habit visibly established.
By day 90Carrying routine operational work with light supervision. A meaningful part of your certification path complete. A clear view, shared with the Chief Technology Officer, of where you are strong and what you attack next.
